Hand annotation

Two hand models, one wrist pose per frame

HOT3D ships each hand twice: once as a UmeTrack skeleton and once as MANO. Both are fitted to the same motion-capture ground truth, so they are posed on exactly the same frames — but their wrist frames are defined differently, which shows up as a standing offset of roughly a hand's width. This page reads the twelve featured Aria payloads and reports what is actually in them.

What the release ships for a hand

UmeTrack

A wrist transform plus a vector of joint angles for the hand skeleton. In this payload: wrist_t (F×3, metres), wrist_q (F×4, w·x·y·z), params (F×22, radians) and a per-frame conf.

MANO

A wrist transform plus pose coefficients of the MANO hand model. In this payload: wrist_t, wrist_q, params (F×15) and conf. The release pairs these with per-participant MANO shape parameters (betas); the betas are a property of the person, not of the frame, and this site's payload does not carry them.

This payload carries the wrist pose and the model parameters, not expanded finger landmarks. Turning the 22 angles or the 15 coefficients into 3D fingertips needs the corresponding hand model and the per-participant hand shape, neither of which is included here. Nothing on this page should be read as a finger-joint position.

Per-recording availability

A hand is annotated on a frame when the released trajectory has an entry for it; everywhere else the payload stores NaN. Separately, the release gives a 2D box per hand per camera, present on the frames where that hand falls inside that camera's view. The two are independent channels rather than one nested inside the other, and the counts below measure them separately.

Why the two models disagree

The gap between the UmeTrack wrist and the MANO wrist is not a tracking error. The two models anchor the wrist at different places in the hand and orient the frame differently, so a roughly constant offset is expected. What matters is that it is steady: the trace above stays flat for as long as the hand is annotated.

UmeTrack joint angles

UmeTrack stores the hand pose as a vector of joint angles alongside the wrist transform. The payload keeps them in release order as an unnamed vector, so the rows below are numbered rather than named; each row is one angle over the whole recording.
