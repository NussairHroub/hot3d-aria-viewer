Three cameras on one pair of glasses

Each Aria recording carries one RGB camera and two monochrome SLAM cameras, and ships its own calibration for that session. This page reads that calibration out of the payload: what each camera sees, how the fisheye model is parameterised, where the cameras sit on the rig and which way they point, and why every image on this site is rotated.

The three streams

One frame from each camera of the selected recording, in the orientation this site uses. The RGB still is the video poster frame; the two SLAM stills are six frames spaced evenly through the recording, stepped together so the pair is from the same instant. The media manifest lists those six files but not their frame numbers, so the time under the stepper is the even spacing rather than a recorded index.

Calibration, per camera

The payload copies the release's camera_models.json verbatim. All three cameras use the same fisheye model, FISHEYE624: one focal length, a principal point, six radial terms, two tangential terms and four thin-prism terms — fifteen numbers in projectionParams, in that order. maxSolidAngle is the largest angle from the optical axis the model is valid over, in radians; read that way it gives the cones below, a few degrees wider than the 110° and 150° fields of view quoted for the Aria RGB and SLAM cameras.

Why every image here is rotated

The Aria sensors are mounted on their side: a camera's image rows run roughly vertically on the head, not horizontally. That is visible in the calibration itself — the angle between each camera's image-up axis and the rig's up axis, computed below, is close to 90° for all three. The release's own images come off the sensor in that native orientation; to look at them, each frame is turned 90° clockwise.

Every video, still and 2D box on this site is in that upright frame. The payload records this explicitly: box_frame is "display" and display_wh gives the size of the upright frame, which is why the SLAM entries are 480×640 while the sensor is 640×480. A page drawing a released box therefore only has to scale it by the width it is displaying at, divided by display_wh.
