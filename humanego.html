HumanEgo keypoint accuracy

HumanEgo's object keypoints, measured against HOT3D's ground truth

HumanEgo learns manipulation from egocentric video by picking twenty points on an object's outline, tracking them, and triangulating them to 3D. HOT3D knows where those surfaces really were, from motion capture, so every one of those points can be scored directly rather than judged by eye. Half of them land within a couple of centimetres of the real surface. The rest are not slightly worse — they are somewhere else entirely, and HumanEgo's own bookkeeping says which ones before any ground truth is consulted.

The share of every triangulated keypoint that lies within a given distance of the intended object's real surface. A method with ordinary noise would give a curve that rises once and flattens near the top. This one rises steeply, stalls, and then creeps on for another two decades of distance, which is the signature of two populations rather than one.

HumanEgo's own eigenvalue separates the two populations

For each object, the triangulator records the first eigenvalue of its point set, a measure of how far those twenty points spread apart. Nothing about the ground truth goes into it. It tracks the point set's physical size closely, and a set spanning metres cannot be a handheld object — so it is a reject filter that costs nothing and can be applied at inference time.

Two measures that fail differently, and agree anyway

Each keypoint is scored twice, by routes that share almost no machinery. One measures distance to the ground-truth surface in 3D, in any direction, with no pixel lookup involved at all. The other measures error along the viewing ray only, by projecting the keypoint into the released fisheye camera and reading the ground-truth depth stored at that pixel. A fault in either route would show up as disagreement between them; instead they rank the keypoints almost identically.

Depth is close to unbiased, which rules out a scale fault

Triangulated depth divided by the true depth at the same pixel. A calibration or scale error would shift the whole distribution off 1.0 and the correction would be a single number. It does not shift, so what is left is scatter in individual reconstructions rather than anything that can be rescaled away.

Why the measured set is 102 recordings

Every recording in the HOT3D v4.0.0 download manifests is present. Three steps reduce that to the set these figures describe, and only the last of them is a property of the method rather than of the release.

These figures describe the easier three-quarters of the set

Only recordings where triangulation produced points are scored. The recordings that produced nothing are not necessarily the easy ones, so the accuracy above is conditioned on the method having worked at all, and is optimistic as a description of the whole release.

The surface is the visible surface

The reference surface is back-projected from the ground-truth depth map at one frame, so it covers the part of the object the camera could see at that instant. A keypoint sitting on a face that was hidden then has nothing nearby to be measured against, which inflates its distance rather than flattering it.

Diverged points are counted, not dropped

A keypoint whose depth falls outside 0.05–20 m is a failed reconstruction, not a measurement with a large error. Quantiles would be meaningless with those included and flattering with them silently removed, so they are excluded from the quantiles and counted against the within-distance shares. Both denominators are stated wherever a share appears.

The masks are ground truth, which is kinder than the real pipeline

HumanEgo normally finds its object with a text-prompted detector. Here the reference masks are written from HOT3D's own object maps instead, so the method is scored on the object it was asked to track rather than on whether a detector picked the right one. Detection error is therefore absent from every number on this page.

One object, one score, twenty times over

The twenty keypoints of an object are not independent samples — they come from one mask, one track and one triangulation. Pooled keypoint figures therefore overstate how much evidence they rest on; the per-object medians in the table above are the honest unit, and there are 199 of those.

Where the numbers came from

The run

HumanEgo's preprocessing stages — keypoint selection, point tracking and multi-view triangulation — over every annotated Aria recording, one job per recording.

The reference

Ground-truth depth and object identity rendered from the release's own object poses and meshes into the released fisheye camera, at the frame HumanEgo triangulates from.

The scoring

Each keypoint scored twice, by the two independent routes described above. This page aggregates that scorer's output and re-estimates nothing.
