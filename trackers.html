Hand-tracker accuracy

Three off-the-shelf hand trackers, measured against mocap

HOT3D annotates hands with motion capture, not a model's guess, so this is an accuracy question rather than an agreement question: run MediaPipe Hands, WiLoR and HaMeR over the Aria RGB frames, project the released ground-truth hands into the same image, measure the gap in pixels. This page reports that measurement, the breakdowns that change how it reads, and where the number is not what it looks like.

Accuracy and coverage in one curve

A single mean hides the trade. The curve sweeps a pixel tolerance and asks what share of scored landmarks lands inside it. Against all evaluable hands a miss fails at every tolerance and the curve tops out at the detection rate; against matched hands only it is conventional PCK, which says nothing about what the tracker skipped.

Denominator

Distance from the camera

Hands are binned by distance from the RGB camera centre to the ground-truth wrist. Distance sets apparent size, so it moves detection and error in opposite directions: a near hand is large and easy to find but its error is measured on a large hand; a far hand is small, easily missed, and cheap in pixels when found.

What the ground truth is

A measurement, not a prediction

HOT3D's hands come from a motion-capture rig. The release stores each hand as a wrist transform plus joint angles, skinned through the participant's own UmeTrack shape profile into 21 landmarks and projected into the RGB camera with the released FISHEYE624 calibration and the MPS headset trajectory. Nothing in that chain is a hand model looking at the picture, which is what makes this an accuracy measurement rather than a disagreement measurement.

Still a fit, not a truth

Mocap gives marker positions; the landmarks are a hand model fitted to them, and the projection adds the calibration's error and the trajectory's. HOT3D publishes no per-frame accuracy figure for its hand annotation, so the floor of this measurement is unknown: these numbers are upper bounds on tracker error, and small differences between trackers are smaller than they look. The one check that exists is geometric — the scorer's projection and rotation land inside the release's own 2D hand boxes on 99.99% of 73,349.

Handedness

An image-space detector labels hands as they appear in the picture, and in an egocentric view the wearer's hands enter from the bottom of the frame and cross, so the label is not free. Each tracker was scored three ways: labels at face value (identity), labels flipped (swapped), and labels ignored in favour of the cheapest one-to-one pairing (geometric). The gap between the best label-based run and the geometric one is the price of the tracker's left/right confusion.

HaMeR's detection rate is partly its detector's

HaMeR is a hand-pose model, not a hand detector: it needs boxes, so its detection rate here is really its box front-end's. The boxes came from ViTPose-H wholebody hand keypoints with a YOLOv8s person detector and a full-image fallback — not the ViTDet/detectron2 front-end of HaMeR's published demo. Another front-end would move the detection rate, the false-positive counts and, through which hands get scored at all, the error. MediaPipe and WiLoR carry their own detectors and ran end to end, so only their numbers are model numbers. The front-end is in each HaMeR prediction npz (opts_json), not in the results file this page reads.
