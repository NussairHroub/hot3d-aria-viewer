Annotation quality

The masks, not the frame count, decide how much of a recording is usable

Every Aria frame carries seven boolean masks per camera stream: the release's own statement about what it will vouch for at that instant. Two recordings of the same length can differ by an order of magnitude in how many frames actually carry a pose. This page reads the masks as they ship, marks where in a recording they fail, and shows how uneven they are across the release.

What each mask asserts

The masks live in masks.<name>.<stream> of each sequence payload, one byte per frame per stream: 1 the assertion holds, 0 it does not, 255 the frame was not annotated on that stream at all. Pass rates on this page are computed over annotated frames only, so a 255 never counts as a pass or as a failure. What a 0 means in the world — an occluded hand, a dropped mocap marker, a blown-out exposure — is not recorded; only the verdict is.

mask 1 means across the three streams, in the 12 featured recordings

The last column is computed from the twelve payloads: for every frame annotated on all three streams, whether rgb, slam_left and slam_right carry the same value. The three pose masks are properties of the timestamp, not of a camera; the exposure and visibility masks are properties of one image and differ between streams.

    The whole Aria release

    index.json carries one number per recording, qa_pass_rgb: the fraction of RGB frames whose qa_pass mask is 1. It is the only quality figure available for the recordings that have no payload on this site, and its distribution is long-tailed — most recordings are nearly clean, and a small group is badly compromised.
