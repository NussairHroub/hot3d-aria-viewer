What the signal is

Each payload carries one gaze block: source, and four per-frame arrays of length F that line up one-to-one with the RGB video. Yaw and pitch are radians, depth is metres. The ranges below are the 1st and 99th percentile over every finite frame of the selected recording, printed in degrees for the angles.

FieldShapeUnit Observed 1–99%What it is

The Central Pupil Frame sits midway between the eyes and moves with the headset, so these angles are relative to the head, not to the world. A yaw per eye plus one shared pitch is the vergence form of the estimate: the two eyes are allowed to differ horizontally, which is what makes a depth recoverable at all.

Vergence

The two eyes do not point the same way: subtracting the right yaw from the left gives the convergence angle, which grows as the fixation point comes closer. The released depth is the same information expressed as a distance, and the two agree to within the precision of a fixed interocular baseline.

Why no gaze dot is drawn on the video

The payload gives gaze only in the Central Pupil Frame. It does not carry the transform from that frame to the device, so the gaze direction cannot be moved into the RGB camera here, and nothing on this site draws a gaze marker on an image.

Projecting it would take three things. First, T_Device_CPF — the pose of the Central Pupil Frame in the device frame, which is part of the Aria device calibration and is not in these payloads. Second, the camera extrinsics T_Device_Camera for camera-rgb, which are in the payload's cameras block, inverted to take a device point into the camera. Third, the fisheye model: the RGB camera is FISHEYE624 at 1408×1408 with 15 projection parameters [f, cx, cy, k1..k6, p1, p2, s1..s4], so the point has to go through that distortion model rather than a pinhole.

Gaze depth matters here too. The Central Pupil Frame and the RGB camera are a few centimetres apart, so a gaze ray from the eyes does not land on one pixel — where it projects depends on how far away the fixation is. The correct target is the 3D point at the released depth along the gaze direction, projected from the camera; treating gaze as a depth-free ray would be wrong by centimetres at arm's length, which at this field of view is tens of pixels.
