Every signal in the Aria half of HOT3D, on one site

HOT3D records people handling objects on a table while wearing Project Aria glasses. Each recording carries three camera streams, motion-capture ground truth for both hands and every object, the headset's own tracking, and per-frame quality masks. These pages put each of those signals where you can look at it directly, rather than reading it out of a CSV.

What you are looking at

Aria recordings carry one 1408×1408 RGB camera and two 640×480 monochrome SLAM cameras, all at 30 frames per second. The ground truth comes from a motion-capture system, so hand and object poses are independent of anything estimated from these images — which is what makes the release useful as a reference rather than only as training data.

Twelve recordings are featured in full here, chosen so that all 33 objects and nine participants appear; every page states which recordings its figures come from. The remaining 186 are listed with their metadata. Of the 198 Aria recordings, 136 ship hand and object ground truth; the other 62 hold back annotations as the benchmark's test split.
